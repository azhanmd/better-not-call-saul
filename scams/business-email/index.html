Business email compromise

Scammers target businesses and anyone who pays suppliers or makes regular transfers. They take over email accounts, phone numbers or video calls, by tricking people or hacking in, to get money sent to them.

How common it is

  • US24,768 complaints to the FBI in 2025, and the second-biggest losses of any crime it tracks: $3 billion.

Warning signs

  • A request to change bank or account details. Check it another way, such as a phone number you already have.
  • A sender's address that doesn't quite match who it claims to be, especially when you read it on a phone.

Who it targets

  • Businesses

Practise spotting it

  • Emerging10 September 2026

    The urgent payment from the CEO

    Why it's emerging: An old trick that AI is making more convincing.

    You work in accounts payable, and your company's email addresses end in @yourcompany.example. An email from the CEO asks you to pay an invoice today.

What to do

  • USCheck any request to pay or change payment details through a second channel, such as a phone number you already have, and make sure the sender's address matches who it claims to be. Source: FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center
  • USPaid a fake request? Contact your bank straight away to ask for a recall, and report it to the FBI at ic3.gov. Source: FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center
