No. 5 of the 6 most common scams

Government and police impersonation

Scammers pose as government officials or the police to get money. They may say you missed jury duty or that your identity was used in a crime, then offer to help, but ask you to pay up front or move all your money to "secure" it.

How common it is

  • US32,424 complaints to the FBI in 2025, almost twice as many as in 2024, with $798 million lost.
  • UKAvon and Somerset Police warned in October 2026 about fraudsters posing as officers, using spoofed police numbers and telling people to move their money to a "safe account".
  • USPeople aged 60 and over made 8,628 of these complaints, almost twice as many as in 2024.

Warning signs

  • !Threats about missed jury duty, or claims that your identity was used in a crime.
  • !Being asked to pay up front, or to move your money somewhere to keep it safe.
  • !Being asked to pay by wire transfer, cryptocurrency, gift cards, or by sending cash or gold. Government and police never ask for this.
  • !An official-looking number on your phone. Numbers can be faked.

Who it targets

  • Anyone
  • Older people
