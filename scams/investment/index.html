Investment scams

Scammers promise big returns with little or no risk, on investments that don't exist, such as cryptocurrency or gold. They often make first contact by text, on social media or through dating apps, build trust, and only then bring up investing.

By the numbers

How common it is

  • US72,984 complaints to the FBI in 2025, and the biggest losses of any crime it tracks: $8.6 billion.
  • UKInvestment scam losses rose 40% in 2025, to a record high.
  • USPeople aged 60 and over lost $3.5 billion to investment scams in 2025.
  • US4,356 of these complaints mentioned AI in 2025, with $632.0 million reported lost. How scammers use AI

Red flags

Warning signs

  • !Big returns with little or no risk. There's no such thing as a guaranteed return.
  • !Someone you met online, by text or on a dating app brings up an investment.
  • !They ask to move your chat to another messaging app.
  • !You're told to pay taxes or fees before you can take your money out.
  • !A celebrity or business leader seems to back it, on social media or on a video call. AI can fake their video and voice.

Who's targeted

Who it targets

  • Anyone
  • Older people

Official advice

What to do

  • UKBefore you invest, check the firm is authorised on the FCA's Firm Checker, and only contact it using the details listed there. Source: Financial Conduct Authority
  • USPaid a fake request? Contact your bank straight away to ask for a recall, and report it to the FBI at ic3.gov. Source: FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center
  • UKLost money or shared your details? Report it to Report Fraud online or on 0300 123 2040. In Scotland, call Police Scotland on 101. Source: Stop! Think Fraud (UK Government)
