The most common scams

Ranked by the number of complaints the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center received in 2025, the latest full year of official figures. Each one explains how the scam works, its warning signs and who it targets, with UK figures where they're available.

  1. 1Phishing
  2. 2Investment
  3. 3Online shopping
  4. 4Tech support
  5. 5Fake officials
  6. 6Business email
  1. 1

    Phishing

    191,561 complaints to the FBI in 2025.

    Scam emails, texts and calls pretend to come from a company or organisation you trust, to get your personal or financial details or your login details.

  2. 2

    Investment scams

    72,984 complaints to the FBI in 2025, and the biggest losses of any crime it tracks: $8.6 billion.

    Scammers promise big returns with little or no risk, on investments that don't exist, such as cryptocurrency or gold. They often make first contact by text, on social media or through dating apps, build trust, and only then bring up investing.

  3. 3

    Online shopping scams

    56,478 complaints to the FBI in 2025 about goods or services that were paid for but never arrived, or weren't paid for.

    Fake shops and sellers take your money for things that never arrive. Links in scam texts, emails and social media posts lead to websites copied from real shops, often with offers that seem too good to be true.

  4. 4

    Tech support scams

    47,794 complaints to the FBI in 2025, with $2.1 billion lost.

    Scammers pose as technical or customer support, such as a computer help desk, your bank, an online shop or a utility company. They try to get you to call them, install software, or hand over control of your device or accounts.

  5. 5

    Government and police impersonation

    32,424 complaints to the FBI in 2025, almost twice as many as in 2024, with $798 million lost.

    Scammers pose as government officials or the police to get money. They may say you missed jury duty or that your identity was used in a crime, then offer to help, but ask you to pay up front or move all your money to "secure" it.

  6. 6

    Business email compromise

    24,768 complaints to the FBI in 2025, and the second-biggest losses of any crime it tracks: $3 billion.

    Scammers target businesses and anyone who pays suppliers or makes regular transfers. They take over email accounts, phone numbers or video calls, by tricking people or hacking in, to get money sent to them.

The FBI also counts extortion and personal data breaches, which aren't covered here. Source: 2025 IC3 Annual Report, FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center.
